Type
Original Zombies map and main quest concept
Players
1–4, co-op
Setting
A natural history museum, connected to the story of Shangri-La

The story

Years after the events of Shangri-La, an archaeological team excavating the ruins of the hidden jungle temple uncovers a strange glowing rock buried in the rubble. It's a leftover shard of the Element 115 meteor, but nobody on the team knows that. To them, it's an unidentified mineral that gives off a faint, steady energy.

The discovery makes headlines around the world, and the media starts calling it a "healing stone." The shard is shipped to a natural history museum so scientists can study it.

Then something goes wrong in the research lab. A piece of equipment malfunctions and fires straight into the shard. Nothing seems to happen, so everyone moves on. But the Element 115 has been supercharged, and its energy slowly spreads through the museum, waking up every fossil and skeleton in the building. Bone grows flesh again, and the exhibits start to move.

Zombies fans will recognize the glowing rock the moment they see it. The scientists have no idea what they've brought inside, and players do. That gap is what makes the opening tense.

Map layout

Players fight their way from the museum entrance to the lab where it all started. The four main rooms form the main path, and each one introduces a bigger, more dangerous threat. As in any Zombies map, players buy doors with points to open new areas. Side rooms branch off the main path for perks, the Mystery Box, and quest items, and loops let players circle the map instead of getting trapped in a dead end.

  1. Entrance Hall. The starting room. Human skeletons from the anthropology exhibits have been reanimated as zombies. A side door leads to the Security Office.
  2. Raptor Gallery. A bigger hall where raptors burst out of their displays. They are faster than zombies and attack in packs. Side rooms: the Gift Shop and the Fossil Prep Room.
  3. Great Hall. The museum's centerpiece room, home to a towering T. rex skeleton, with the Planetarium off to one side. An upper balcony loops all the way around the T. rex, so players can keep moving during the boss fight. This is where the T. rex comes to life, with raptors attacking alongside it.
  4. Research Lab. The source of the outbreak, where the supercharged shard is kept. It stays sealed until players have built the weapon, which makes it the finale.

The main quest

Between rounds, players collect pieces of technology scattered across the museum's side rooms. Put together, they form a weapon powerful enough to destroy the shard. Once the weapon is built, the Research Lab unlocks, and players push through to destroy the shard. Its energy cuts out instantly, and everything that came back to life collapses into bones in front of them.

Reward

Players keep the weapon they built for the rest of the game.

Hidden story

Audio logs and lab notes hidden around the museum let players piece together what happened. In one, a scientist records that the rock's readings "match nothing on the periodic table." It's a nod for longtime fans, who know exactly what that means.

Design notes

  • Escalating threat. Each room introduces a new kind of enemy: slow zombies, then fast raptors in packs, then a giant boss. Players learn each threat before the next one is layered on top.
  • A setting that tells the story. A museum already has its enemies on display. Players walk past the skeletons and fossils before they wake up, so the danger is visible from the start.
  • Built on existing lore. Element 115 is what reanimates the dead in Zombies, so the outbreak needs no new explanation. Linking it to Shangri-La rewards longtime players for spotting the connection.
  • Room to move. The side rooms and the balcony loop keep the map from being a straight corridor, so players always have a way to circle around a horde.
  • A satisfying ending. Destroying the shard turns every living exhibit back into bones at once. It's a clear, visual payoff that tells players they won.
